Core Game Mechanics: Epoch of the Realms1. Match Objective & StructureThe game is a strategic, best-of-three-rounds card battler. To win a match, a player must secure victory in at least two out of the 3 Rounds. A round is won by controlling the majority of the 3 Locations on the board. You control a location by having the highest total Power on your side of that location when the round resolves.2. Deck & Hand ManagementDeck Size: Each player builds a deck of exactly 30 cards. Cards are categorized by Faction (e.g., Elarion Empire, Elderveld, Kalthrone, Horde of the Carrion) and Rarity (Mortal, Cursed, Arcane, Forbidden, Mythic, Relic, Divine).   Starting Hand: Players begin Round 1 with exactly 5 cards in their hand.Hand Limit: You can never hold more than 5 cards at any given time.Draw Mechanic: There is no "start of turn" draw phase. Instead, whenever you play a card onto the board, you immediately draw 1 random card from your deck to replace it.3. The Battlefield & Capacity
The board is divided into 3 distinct Locations drawn from a randomized pool (e.g., The Abyss, Labyrinth, Battlefield). Each location features its own environmental modifiers, buffs, or hazards that alter gameplay.   Standard Slots: Each player can place a maximum of 8 Standard Cards at each location.Chaos Slot: Each player has 1 dedicated Chaos Slot per location, allowing for a total of 9 cards per location per player.4. Card Types & AbilitiesThere is no "Energy" or "Mana" cost system in the game; you may play any card from your hand at any time.Standard Cards (Vanilla & Ability): Regular units deployed to the 8 standard slots. Vanilla cards rely purely on base Power, while Ability cards feature keywords like On Reveal, Ongoing, Muster, Medic, or Spy.   Chaos Cards: Highly volatile cards placed exclusively in the Chaos Slot. These cards typically trigger massive RNG effects, such as introducing a percentage chance to "burn" (destroy) cards at that location based on the combined power on the board (e.g., Maneater, Vaelthar, Terror of Worlds).   5. Turn Progression (The Action Phase)Players alternate taking turns, similar to a tennis match. During your turn, you may play as many cards as you wish (up to your 5-card hand limit and board space).The "Next" Action: Once you have placed your desired cards for that specific turn, you click Next. This shifts the initiative to your opponent, allowing them to respond to your plays.The "Pass" Action: If you are satisfied with your total board state—or want to bait your opponent into over-committing their cards—you may click Pass. Passing signifies you are locking your board and will play no more cards for the entire duration of the current round.Once a player passes, the opponent may continue playing cards and clicking "Next" by themselves until they also choose to Pass, run out of board space, or run out of cards.6. Round Resolution & ScoringPower Calculation: As soon as both players click Pass, the round immediately ends. The game calculates the total Power of all cards on each player's side of the 3 locations.Location Victory: The player with the highest Total Power at a specific location wins that location, scoring 1 Point.Round Victory: The player who wins 2 out of the 3 locations (scoring 2 or 3 Points) wins the Round.Board Reset: At the end of the round, the board is completely cleared, sending all standard played cards to the graveyard—unless specifically protected by a location effect like Future (which moves cards to the next round) or Valhalla (which resurrects destroyed cards). Players keep their current hand of 5 cards going into the next round.
